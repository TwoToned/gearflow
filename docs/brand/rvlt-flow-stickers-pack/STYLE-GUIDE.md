# RVLT Flow Sticker and Wordmark Design Guide

Visual style and practical use

![A selection from the current collection, combining production gear, crew language and the RVLT Flow patch.](images/style-01.png)

A selection from the current collection, combining production gear, crew language and the RVLT Flow patch.

The RVLT Flow artwork brings the world of live production into the brand: cases, tools, show cues and the everyday objects a crew recognises. The style is simple, bright and a little playful, with enough restraint to sit comfortably beside a working app.

This guide explains how the illustrations and wordmarks fit together, why the colours and shapes were chosen, and how to use the files on screens or prepare them for physical stickers. It is intended for anyone designing with the collection or extending it.

The collection has 18 illustrations and 7 wordmarks, each supplied in two SVG formats: 50 files in total. The artwork shown is the version 9 gallery set. Sizes and placement guidance below are recommended starting points for this art system; check them in the finished layout.

Current artwork and downloads: [RVLT Flow gear gallery](https://rvlt-gear-drafts.jayden73358.chatgpt.site)

## The illustration collection

![The 18 current illustrations. Colour, silhouette and optical scale work together to give the set a consistent visual weight.](images/style-02.png)

### A vocabulary drawn from the job

The road case, van and gaff tape establish the production setting. The microphone, monitor, speaker, moving head and console cover show equipment. The calendar, clipboard, batteries, wrench and AAA pass bring in preparation and crew work.

The walkie-talkie, coffee mug, ticket and curtains add the social and theatrical side of a show. The pelican is a personal reference within the collection. Its role is a recognisable, slightly unexpected character; no additional story or mascot identity is assumed.

### Choosing the right subject

Match the artwork to the surrounding topic: a case or van near logistics, a clipboard near preparation, curtains near a show announcement. Use the coffee mug or pelican when warmth and personality are the main reason for the image. Keep the subject understandable without a caption.

## How the drawings are built

| Element | Current artwork |
| --- | --- |
| Illustration canvas | 128 × 128 SVG units |
| Sticker shape | Circle at 64,64 with radius 57; or 114 × 114 rounded square at 7,7 with radius 23 |
| Line and edge | About 3.4 units for the illustration; 3 units for the cream sticker border |
| Ends and joins | Rounded line caps and joins; small solid details only where they help recognition |
| Depth | A solid shadow offset 2 units right and 4 units down |
| Word patches | Individual silhouettes, 4-unit cream edges and tightly cropped viewBoxes |

### Balance by eye

A narrow walkie-talkie, an open tape roll and a diagonal microphone should feel equally present. Give each its own scale and position. Start with the main subject occupying roughly 55–70% of the sticker width or height, then adjust for the amount of ink and the direction of the silhouette. The curtains deliberately frame the whole interior.

Keep clear gaps between separate strokes. Remove a line when it repeats information the silhouette already gives. Let a rounded handle, a slight tilt or an arched word add character; artificial wobble and extra symbols are not needed.

## Colour choices

Espresso gives the bright stickers a dark stage to sit on. Cream holds the collection together through its outlines and lettering; the hard shadow makes each piece feel like a separate object. Flat colour keeps the drawings immediate and easy to reproduce.

| Colour | Current examples | Why it works here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RVLT red | Road case, curtains, On air, RVLT | Brand recognition and show energy |
| Amber | Tape, moving head, mug, Standby | A warm note among the cooler equipment colours |
| Green | Monitor, walkie-talkie, Go! | Liveliness; a natural cue association for Go! |
| Blue | Van, clipboard, Load in | A practical anchor for transport and preparation |
| Purple | Microphone, speaker, Crew | Connects audio gear with the expressive crew badge |
| Coral | Wrench, console, ticket, Show time | Warmth and variety alongside the stronger brand red |
| Teal | Calendar, pelican | A clear cool contrast to amber and coral |
| Pink | AAA pass | Makes the access pass easy to pick out |
| Lime | Batteries | A distinct accent for a small, repeated object |

These associations describe the collection; they are not a new status system. The same hue can appear on unrelated subjects. In app screens, keep existing status and module colours authoritative. For a new decorative cluster, begin with one dominant colour and one or two supporting colours. Use espresso lettering on the brighter patch fills; retain the supplied cream lettering where designed.

Baloo 2 gives the longer phrases their rounded character. Archivo gives Go! and RVLT a firmer emphasis. Letters are converted to vector paths and placed along gentle curves, preserving their shapes. Keep the supplied letter spacing, line breaks and proportions when using the finished artwork.

## Using the art in layouts

### Use a small number of deliberate accents

On marketing pages, start with one main sticker and one or two smaller companions around a heading, hero image or final call to action. A cluster should have a clear focal point. Repeating a sticker at equal sizes across every section makes the artwork feel like wallpaper.

In the app, use a single illustration for onboarding, an empty state or a positive completion moment. Keep decorative art outside tables, controls, alerts and compliance messages. The pelican and show patches work best where personality helps the user feel welcomed or recognise the brand.

### Space and size recommendations

| Decision | Starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Clear space | Leave about 10% of the sticker’s shorter visible dimension between its edge and text or controls. |
| Rotation | Use the current gallery’s small tilts, usually 2–5 degrees. Keep curtains upright. Avoid mirroring words or recognisable equipment. |
| Digital scale | Try 96–240 px for illustrated stickers and 160–320 px for word patches. Below 64 px, inspect every detail; prefer a dedicated UI icon for small controls. |
| Cropping and overlap | Overlap backing shapes sparingly in collages. Keep the subject and every letter visible. Crop decoration at a page edge only when recognition survives. |
| Responsive layout | Reduce the number and size of stickers on phones. Preserve reading order and the space around buttons. |

Keep the SVG aspect ratio intact. A wide label and a taller Show time badge need different widths; making every file the same CSS width will not give them the same visual weight. Check the painted outline and its shadow rather than judging the empty canvas. Motion is optional; a still composition should work on its own.

## Choosing and preparing the files

| File ending | Use |
| --- | --- |
| -sticker.svg | Illustration, colour backing, cream edge and hard shadow. Use for a ready-made digital sticker. |
| -line.svg | Transparent cream illustration. Use for quieter decoration or prepare a deliberate recoloured variant. |
| -patch.svg | Wordmark with its shaped background, border and shadow. |
| -wordmark.svg | Transparent outlined lettering. Most are cream; RVLT Flow keeps its red and cream pairing. |

### Web and digital use

Keep SVG as the master format and retain its viewBox. Scale proportionally; use width with height set to auto, or contain the image within its display area. The sticker shadow is already included, so an extra blurred CSS shadow changes the style. For presentation or social tools that need PNG, export at the final size or larger from the SVG. [4]

The current line artwork has an explicit cream colour inside the file. Changing a parent CSS colour will not recolour an SVG loaded through an image element. Edit a copy or prepare an inline SVG variant deliberately. On light backgrounds, use the full sticker or a reviewed espresso line variant.

Pure decoration should use empty alternative text, alt="". Give an informative image an appropriate text alternative, and keep important instructions as real text. A decorative On air patch is not a reliable live-status indicator. [3]

### Physical stickers and patches

Start a print proof around 50–75 mm wide for individual designs, then inspect the lettering and smallest gaps at actual size. This is a design recommendation, not a tested minimum. The current SVGs have no production cut paths or bleed; they are artwork masters.

Work with the printer to set the cut contour, safe margin, bleed and colour profile. Use the cream border as an intentional printed margin; decide whether to remove the screen shadow or print it as part of the design. Confirm the cream and bright fills on the chosen stock. Transparent vinyl needs a deliberate white-ink plan. For embroidered patches, ask the maker to simplify small stitches and gaps and produce a sample.

## The creative process

The direction developed through repeated viewing and specific corrections. The strongest changes came from identifying what made an object recognisable, removing competing detail and judging every piece beside the rest of the set. The final balance matters more than how elaborate the drawing is.

| Feedback during development | Design lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Gear felt too detailed or mechanically generated | Reduce repeated marks, use clearer silhouettes and study designed icon families. The microphone was rebuilt from a consistent reference family. |
| Tape read as a Q | Keep the roll, inner opening and loose end visually distinct. Recognition takes priority over a clever continuous line. |
| The walkie-talkie felt too wide | Correct the object’s proportions before adding details. |
| The coffee mug needed character but the bolt was unwanted | Let the tapered body, curved handle, rim and steam carry the personality. An unrelated symbol is not a substitute for a better drawing. |
| The pelican did not read as a pelican | Use bird references to establish the long bill, throat pouch, curved neck and feet before simplifying. |
| Patches needed whimsy and Show time felt the wrong size | Vary the outer shape and baseline, then crop to the actual artwork and balance each patch individually. |

### A repeatable method for new artwork

1  Choose a subject that belongs to the crew’s world. Write down the two or three features that make it recognisable. For equipment, check the actual object; for a bird, check its anatomy.

2  Draw the silhouette first. Add only the details that resolve ambiguity. Compare its line weight, corner treatment and density with the existing family.

3  Choose a backing shape and palette colour. Fit by eye beside a narrow, a wide and a diagonal illustration. For a word patch, let the phrase guide the shape and rhythm.

4  Review at the intended size and again smaller. Remove awkward tangencies, tiny gaps, accidental symbols and unnecessary decorative strokes. Check that typography has comfortable edge clearance.

5  Export both versions, preserve source credits and inspect the actual SVGs in their intended layout. Check the download bundle and visible gallery together so everyone is reviewing the same artwork.

## Keeping the collection consistent

### The review pass

Before adding a design, ask whether someone familiar with production would name the object without its label. Check the silhouette in one colour, then compare the sticker with its neighbours. A design should still read when its rotation and coloured background are removed.

Inspect letter counters, edge spacing and the gaps between strokes at the smallest intended size. Avoid decorative bolts, rays, faux wear or extra controls unless they are necessary to the subject. Do not distort the source artwork to force a fit. Keep the simple shapes, rounded strokes, flat fills and hard shadow consistent.

### Brand relationship

This collection is an expressive part of RVLT Flow. Its broad colour palette is for art and decorative patches. It does not replace the app’s semantic colour rules, operational icons or typography for readable interface text.

The RVLT Flow patch is a stylised artwork lockup, not a replacement master logo. It follows the newer supplied doc’s Archivo direction for RVLT and the rounded Baloo 2 wordmark treatment. Earlier references also contain a BC Alphapipe RVLT mark; the current patch uses its own Archivo lettering. Use the approved primary logo where formal brand identification is required.
